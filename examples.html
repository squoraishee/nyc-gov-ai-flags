NYC DOT to Use New Cutting-Edge Sensors to Better Understand How New Yorkers Use Their Streets and to Inform Safer Street Design
Small, privacy-protective street activity sensors count pedestrians, cyclists, buses, and vehicles and assess patterns in how people use the streets
Sensors were previously tested at 20 intersections along a variety of corridors in all five boroughs and will expand to 100 locations citywide
An example of data collected by a street activity sensor, with mapping of movement by pedestrians, cyclists, and cars at an intersection.
NEW YORK — New York City Department of Transportation (NYC DOT) Commissioner Mike Flynn today announced the use of new cutting-edge sensors (with video here) to better understand how New Yorkers use their streets and to inform safer street design. First piloted in 2023 , these small, privacy-protective street activity sensors will count pedestrians, cyclists, buses, and vehicles, and assess patterns in how people use the streets. Sensors will be installed at approximately 80 additional locations across the five boroughs to accelerate data-driven street safety improvements.
"Safer street design starts with understanding what is actually happening on the street," said NYC DOT Commissioner Mike Flynn. "These high-tech sensors will help us evaluate how people are walking, biking, and driving so we can design safer streets and encourage safer behavior."
Building on the successful pilot, NYC DOT will this year scale up a technology that has already transformed how the agency measures traffic activity. Initially installed at 20 locations, the sensors replaced traditional manual traffic counts with continuous, real-time data collection—providing a far more comprehensive picture of how pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles move through city streets.
Mounted on NYC DOT street infrastructure, the sensors anonymously analyze street activity. In addition to counting different types of road users, sensors can measure speeds, capture turning movements, and map how different users move. For example, the sensors could identify areas where pedestrians are crossing mid-block instead of at crosswalks. This could help identify locations where a mid-block crosswalk would be beneficial.
Traditional planning methods have previously relied on short-term, labor-intensive counts. The sensors operate continuously, capturing changes in travel patterns by time of day, season, and street design. Designed with privacy as a core value, video footage is processed in real time and immediately discarded, with only anonymous data retained. Faces and license plates are deliberately obscured in any video footage.
By analyzing detailed data on how streets are used, the agency expects to use the sensors to:
Evaluate the effectiveness of street redesigns and safety projects
Identify high-risk locations before crashes occur using "near-miss" data
Better allocate street space among pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles
Improve access to transit, loading zones, and local businesses
Build a dataset that measures how pedestrian and cycling volumes change across the city and over time.
NYC DOT Traffic Operations worker installing a new activity sensor in Greenpoint, Brooklyn.
The sensors, provided by Viva, will be installed in a diverse range of corridors—from busy commercial streets to residential neighborhoods, ensuring that sensor data reflects the full complexity of New York City's transportation network.

About NYC Department of Cultural Affairs
The New York City Department of Cultural Affairs (DCLA) is dedicated to supporting and strengthening New York City’s vibrant cultural life. DCLA works to promote and advocate for quality arts programming and to articulate the contribution made by the cultural community to the City’s vitality. The Department represents and serves non-profit cultural organizations involved in the visual, literary, and performing arts; public-oriented science and humanities institutions including zoos, botanical gardens, and historic and preservation societies; and creative artists at all skill levels who live and work within the City’s five boroughs. DCLA also provides donated materials for arts programs offered by the public schools and cultural and social service groups, and commissions permanent works of public art at City-funded construction projects throughout the five boroughs. SAFEGUARDING NEW YORK CITY’S ESSENTIAL SERVICES
WHEREAS, the City of New York is entrusted with the continuous delivery of essential services upon which all New Yorkers depend, including public safety, health, transportation, water, education, social services, the administration of justice, and communication of critical information to the public; and
WHEREAS, the continuity of these essential services is foundational to the safety, welfare, and economic vitality of all New Yorkers, and must be sustained regardless of the nature, scale, or duration of any disruption; and
WHEREAS, effective continuity of essential services requires the integration of Continuity of Operations (“COOP”), Continuity of Government (“COG”), and Mission Assurance across all City agencies, coordinated at the most senior levels of City government; and
WHEREAS, sustained coordination among City agencies, including those responsible for emergency management, technology, and physical assets, is essential to maintaining continuity readiness before, during, and after disruptions; and
WHEREAS, Executive Order No. 107, issued October 2, 2007, established the City’s initial framework for Continuity of Operations planning, and while that Order advanced important foundational work, the intervening years have demonstrated the need for a more comprehensive and updated policy to address evolving threats; and
WHEREAS, it is the policy of this Administration to ensure that City agencies institute plans to maintain the delivery of essential services to all New Yorkers without interruption during any emergency, disruption, or threat thereof; and
WHEREAS, to advance this policy, the City shall establish and sustain a coordinated, proactive, and comprehensive framework for continuity planning, risk monitoring, and operational readiness across all City agencies;
NOW, THEREFORE, by the power vested in me as the Mayor of the City of New York, it is hereby ordered:
Section 1. Definitions . For the purposes of this Order, the following terms have the following meanings:
a. “Citywide Priority Essential Services” means those essential services identified through an interagency process whose disruption would produce the most significant cascading impacts for New Yorkers and for the functioning of City government.
b. “Continuity of Operations” (“COOP”) means the ability of a City agency to maintain or rapidly resume its essential services during and after a disruption to normal operations.
c. “Continuity of Government” (“COG”) means the preservation of the constitutional and lawful authority and leadership of City government, including lines of succession, delegations of authority, and the safeguarding of vital records during and after a disruption.
d. “Mission Assurance” means the proactive and continuous process of identifying, managing, and mitigating risks to the City’s ability to deliver essential services, encompassing planning, monitoring, coordination, and resource alignment across all phases of a disruption.
§ 2. Continuity and Mission Assurance Team . There is hereby established a Continuity and Mission Assurance Team (“CMAT”), which shall serve as the City’s senior interagency body for COOP, COG and mission assurance policy, planning, coordination, and oversight. The CMAT shall:
a. Be chaired by the Deputy Mayor for Operations, or their designee. The Chair shall be responsible for setting the strategic direction of the City’s continuity and mission assurance programs, convening the CMAT, and ensuring continuity priorities are reflected in citywide policy, resource allocation, and performance management.
b. Be composed of senior representatives of New York City Emergency Management (“NYCEM”), the Department of Citywide Administrative Services (“DCAS”), the Office of Management and Budget (“OMB”), and the Office of Technology and Innovation (“OTI”), and any additional agency or office as determined by the Chair. Each representative shall have sufficient authority within their respective agency or office to commit resources, share information, and make or elevate policy and operational decisions.
i. NYCEM shall serve as the lead coordinating agency for the City’s continuity and mission assurance programs. NYCEM shall be responsible for developing and maintaining citywide continuity policies, standards, and guidance; administering the City’s enterprise continuity planning software; supporting priority interagency readiness activities as designated by the CMAT; and staffing and supporting the operations of the CMAT.
ii. DCAS shall provide functional leadership and subject matter expertise to the CMAT for facilities, fleet, logistics, and personnel continuity.
iii. OMB shall serve as an advisory partner to ensure that continuity planning and readiness are reflected in the City’s fiscal, capital, and performance management processes.
iv. OTI shall provide functional leadership and subject matter expertise to the CMAT for information technology, cybersecurity, and communications continuity.
v. The Chair may direct additional agencies and offices to participate or support specific CMAT initiatives, working groups, or coordination activities as necessary.
c. Meet on a regular basis, no less than quarterly, and more frequently as conditions require.
§ 3. Agency Responsibilities and Coordination .
a. Mayoral agencies shall:
i. Identify essential services and ensure that such services reflect any significant functional or operational changes to the agency over time.
ii. Maintain a COOP, updated and exercised no less than once annually, that addresses both the continuity of the agency’s essential services and its role in sustaining the continuity of City government. Such plans shall be consistent with citywide standards and guidance issued pursuant to this Order and shall be updated annually by December 31st of each calendar year.
iii. Designate a senior official responsible for continuity planning and readiness within such agency, to serve as the Executive Sponsor of the agency’s COOP, and to coordinate the agency team responsible for the development and maintenance of the agency’s COOP.
b. The Deputy Mayor for Operations and NYCEM shall take all steps necessary, consistent with applicable law, to facilitate and encourage participation by non-mayoral City agencies and other entities affiliated with the City in the initiatives set forth in this Order.
§ 4. Citywide Priority Essential Services . NYCEM, in coordination with the CMAT, shall establish and maintain a framework for identifying Citywide Priority Essential Services, which shall receive heightened attention in planning and monitoring. The CMAT shall periodically review and update the identification of such services to reflect evolving risks and City priorities.
§ 5. Accountability and Reporting . Each agency shall maintain and annually update a COOP plan consistent with citywide continuity planning standards. Each agency also will conduct at least one exercise annually to test its COOP plan. The CMAT, acting through NYCEM, shall be responsible for enforcing agency compliance of both the annual COOP plan update and the required annual exercise, and shall report on agency compliance as CMAT determines appropriate.
§ 6. Executive Order No. 107, dated October 2, 2007, is hereby REVOKED.
§ 7. This order shall take effect immediately.

ESTABLISHING SEPTEMBER 11 AS A CITYWIDE DAY OF REMEMBRANCE AND SERVICE
WHEREAS, on September 11, 2001, the City of New York suffered an immeasurable tragedy that claimed the lives of thousands of New Yorkers and left an indelible mark on the City and the nation; and
WHEREAS, amidst the terror attacks of September 11th, thousands of first responders ran toward danger so others could run toward safety, including members of the Fire Department of the City of New York (“FDNY”), the New York City Police Department (“NYPD”), the Port Authority Police Department (“PAPD”), the New York State Office of Tax Enforcement (“OTE”), the New York State Office of Court Administration (“OCA”), the Federal Bureau of Investigation (“FBI”), the New York Fire Patrol (“FPNY”), and the United States Secret Service (“USSS”); and
WHEREAS, 343 members of the FDNY and 71 law enforcement officers, including 23 NYPD members of service, 37 PAPD officers, four members of the OTE, three OCA officers, one member of the FBI, one member of the FPNY, and one member of the USSS, made the ultimate sacrifice in the line of duty here in New York City on September 11th; and
WHEREAS, the sacrifice of New York City’s first responders has continued to be felt in the years since the attacks, as hundreds of first responders and rescue and recovery workers have died from illnesses associated with their service and exposure at the World Trade Center site; and
WHEREAS, New Yorkers across all five boroughs continue to grieve the loved ones who went to work and never returned home, the first responders who answered the call, and the many who fell ill in the aftermath; and
WHEREAS, nearly 60,000 New York City employees from agencies across City government participated in the rescue, recovery, cleanup, and restoration efforts following the attacks, supporting the City’s response through search and rescue, emergency response, medical care, debris removal, public health services, infrastructure support, logistics, and other essential operations; and
WHEREAS, the effects of September 11th continue to be felt throughout New York City by survivors, victims’ families, responders, City employees, rescue and recovery workers, and communities affected by the attacks and their aftermath; and
WHEREAS, September 11th is an enduring part of the history of New York City, and the City has a responsibility to preserve the memory of those who were lost, honor those who responded, recognize the families who continue to carry their loss, and ensure that future generations, including New Yorkers born after September 11, 2001, understand the events, sacrifices, and lasting impact of that day and its aftermath; and
WHEREAS, in the aftermath of September 11th, New Yorkers demonstrated extraordinary solidarity and commitment to one another through acts of service, volunteerism, mutual aid, and support for those in need; and
WHEREAS, September 11th is recognized nationally as a Day of Service and Remembrance, encouraging Americans to honor those who were lost by serving their communities and helping others; and
WHEREAS, it is fitting that the City of New York formally recognize September 11th each year as a day of remembrance and service, honoring those who were lost, the first responders who answered the call, the families who have carried their loss, and the many New Yorkers who came together in service to one another;
NOW, THEREFORE, by the power vested in me as Mayor of the City of New York, it is hereby ordered:
Section 1. September 11th Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service . September 11th of each year is hereby designated as a Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service. The Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service shall be observed as a day to:
a. Remember and honor the victims of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks;
b. Honor the first responders who responded to the World Trade Center and made the ultimate sacrifice, and those first responders and rescue and recovery workers who have since died from illnesses associated with their service and exposure at the World Trade Center site;
c. Recognize and honor the families and loved ones of those who died on September 11th and those who have since died from illnesses related to the September 11th attacks and cleanup, and acknowledge the enduring loss they have carried;
d. Recognize survivors and communities whose lives continue to be affected by September 11th; and
e. Recognize the essential contributions of City employees who participated in the rescue, recovery, cleanup, public health, infrastructure, and restoration efforts following the attacks.
§ 2. City Observance .
a. The Office of the Mayor shall coordinate the annual Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service.
b. Mayoral agencies, consistent with their respective missions and operational needs, shall be encouraged to participate in commemorative activities.
c. City agencies and offices are encouraged to conduct appropriate commemorative programming, including memorial ceremonies, moments of silence, educational programming, displays, and other activities honoring those who were lost, first responders and other responders, their families, survivors, and affected communities.
d. City agencies shall, where appropriate and consistent with applicable law, display the flags of the United States and the City of New York in accordance with applicable federal, State, and City requirements.
e. The Office of the Mayor shall designate one or more times during the Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service for a Citywide moment of silence in honor of those who lost their lives on September 11, 2001, and those who have since died from illnesses associated with the attacks. Mayoral agencies shall be encouraged to participate in such moments of silence and to facilitate the participation of employees and members of the public where operationally feasible.
§ 3. Day of Service . The Office of the Mayor, in coordination with NYC Service and other agencies determined by the Mayor, shall promote opportunities for New Yorkers to participate in volunteer and community service activities as part of the Citywide Day of Remembrance and Service. Mayoral agencies shall be encouraged, where operationally feasible and consistent with applicable law, to organize or participate in service activities benefiting New Yorkers and communities in need, provided that such participation does not negatively impact the delivery of services to the public.
§ 4. Effective Date . This order shall take effect immediately.

Funding Will Help Community Organizations Hire 500 Additional Peer Specialists and Expand Outreach Citywide
NEW YORK — Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani today announced a $12 million investment to expand access to life-saving recovery services for New Yorkers at risk of overdose. The funding will support the certification and employment of peer specialists — people with a history of substance-use disorder who help others in recovery — and strengthen direct outreach efforts across the five boroughs. Over four years, the investment is expected to create 500 new peer specialist positions through community-based organizations citywide.
"Every New Yorker deserves access to care, dignity and support, no matter where they are in their recovery journey. "Peer specialists understand what recovery looks like because they've lived it themselves, and their leadership will help more New Yorkers find care, stay connected and build stable futures. NEW YORK, NY – TODAY, Mayor Mamdani announced that LinkNYC terminals across the city are playing a public service announcement (PSA) directly urging homeless New Yorkers to come indoors and connect with city services as dangerously cold temperatures continue.
The video also provides guidance for New Yorkers who are concerned about someone they see outside, explaining how to request help using the LinkNYC kiosks. Anyone can use the terminals to make a free call to 311 or connect directly to emergency services by pressing the red 911 button.
“With temperatures this low, being outside can be life-threatening. These kiosks are everywhere, they’re free to use, and they can be a lifeline,” said Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani.
"LinkNYC is proud to partner with the Mamdani administration to help keep our most vulnerable neighbors safe, warm, and informed," said Nick Colvin, CEO of LinkNYC. "During severe winter weather, it's critical that New Yorkers know they can use any LinkNYC kiosk to connect with shelters, food pantries, warming centers, and 311 for free.”
Over the next several days, the PSA is expected to receive millions of impressions. Every LinkNYC terminal includes a dial pad for free calls to 311, as well as a direct connection to 911 for emergencies.
The PSA is part of a broader, whole-of-government effort to bring people in from the cold and connect them with shelter and ongoing support. Since the beginning of extreme winter weather, the city has made nearly 1,000 placements into shelter.
Earlier this week, the Mayor joined Department of Social Services (DSS) Commissioner Molly Wasow Park to announce the opening a new Safe Haven shelter, which was fast-tracked in response to the cold snap. The administration has also expanded single-room-occupancy style units designed for New Yorkers who have been hesitant to use traditional shelter options.
The city continues to deploy mobile warming units staffed with clinicians and services, along with enhanced warming centers, to ensure multiple pathways off the street and out of the cold.
“Today’s announcement is about meeting people where they are. If you’re outside, we want you inside. If you’re worried about someone else, help is just a call away. We are here, and we are ready to help,” Mamdani said.

NUEVA YORK – Veinticinco años después de los ataques del 11 de septiembre, la Ciudad de Nueva York abrió sus propios archivos sobre lo que había en el aire del Bajo Manhattan y sobre lo que su gobierno sabía.
El alcalde Zohran Kwame Mamdani anunció hoy la publicación de más de 170.000 páginas de documentos municipales y el fin de dos demandas presentadas por la organización 9/11 Health Watch. El acuerdo pone fin a una disputa legal que llevaba años y giraba en torno a los mismos documentos sobre la calidad del aire y los riesgos para la salud.
Para muchas familias, lo que está en juego es concreto. Miles de socorristas y sobrevivientes necesitan comprobar que estuvieron en la Zona de Exposición de la Ciudad de Nueva York para poder acceder a dos programas federales creados bajo la Ley Zadroga: el Programa de Salud del World Trade Center y el Fondo de Compensación para las Víctimas del 11 de septiembre. La Administración Mamdani asignará personal al Departamento de Servicios Administrativos de la Ciudad (DCAS) y a las Escuelas Públicas de la Ciudad de Nueva York (NYCPS) para ayudarles a localizar esos documentos y trabajará para que más personas soliciten ambos programas.
El portal ya está disponible en nyc.gov/sept11docs. Incluye el Memorándum Harding, muy citado a lo largo de los años; las llamadas “68 cajas” de documentos que no aparecieron hasta 2025, pese a varias solicitudes bajo la Ley de Libertad de Información (FOIL); y un conjunto de documentos sobre el edificio 7 del World Trade Center.
El Departamento Jurídico trabajó directamente con 9/11 Health Watch para incluir en esta primera entrega buena parte de lo que los demandantes pedían. Lo único que se omitió fue lo estrictamente necesario, sobre todo la información que identifica a las personas. El portal se irá actualizando durante los próximos 12 meses, conforme el Departamento Jurídico revise más archivos, y la Ciudad espera publicar bastante más material en ese plazo.
Socorristas, funcionarios electos y familias de personas que murieron por enfermedades relacionadas con el 11 de septiembre llevan varias administraciones pidiendo estos documentos. El presupuesto del año fiscal 2027 destina $34 millones a la creación y el mantenimiento del portal.
“Lo mínimo que nuestra ciudad les debe a las familias, los sobrevivientes y los socorristas cuyas vidas cambiaron para siempre el 11 de septiembre es transparencia y rendición de cuentas. Por eso me enorgullece que nuestra administración haya lanzado el primer portal público de la Ciudad dedicado a los efectos ambientales y de salud del 11 de septiembre, y que hayamos llegado a un acuerdo en estas demandas”, dijo el alcalde Zohran Mamdani. “Durante demasiado tiempo, los neoyorquinos han tenido que pelear por documentos que debieron estar a su alcance desde el principio. Este portal es parte del compromiso permanente de nuestra administración de honrar a quienes perdimos, cuidar a quienes viven con secuelas de salud y darles a miles de neoyorquinos la respuesta que llevan años pidiendo”.
“Durante años, los sobrevivientes y las familias han pedido más transparencia a su gobierno”, dijo el Corporation Counsel Steven Banks. “Los escuchamos y hoy lanzamos este portal para dar acceso al público a información y documentos sobre la respuesta del gobierno de la Ciudad a los ataques del 11 de septiembre. Son documentos que nunca antes estuvieron disponibles y que resultan esenciales para entender qué hizo y qué decidió la Ciudad, tanto en los meses posteriores al 11 de septiembre como en los años siguientes”.

Prop 1: Speed Up Timeline for Using Public Space – Including Outdoor Dining, Ramps and Benches
Cut the eight-month outdoor dining application process by 75% (to 2 months)
Save small businesses up to $1,800 on application costs and thousands of dollars of staff time
The Commission’s proposal would modify the revocable consent process that determines the use of public space by combining the sidewalk café and roadway dining revocable consent process into a single application and modernizing public notice requirements.
Prop 2 and related non-Charter reforms: Reform the Contracting Process to Improve Procurements, including for Small Business and Nonprofits
Ballot prop will save up to 30 days on procurements
Internal reforms to cut qualified nonprofit procurement time by more than half (13 months to 6)
The Commission’s proposal would reduce burdens on small businesses and nonprofits by simplifying the pre-contracting questionnaire and requiring quarterly meetings of the Procurement Policy Board (PPB) to ensure consistent and transparent regulation of the City’s procurement system. The proposal would also allow the Mayor to delegate certain contract approvals to agencies, make permanent a public comment period instead of a public hearing for contract awards and streamline the issuance of the client services plan.
Beyond ballot props, the City will also begin to move nonprofit Council discretionary awards from procurements to grants, an approach that in pilot stage has shown to cut time to payment from 10 to six months. The City will also begin to competitively procure umbrella agreements with nonprofits to create pools of qualified, pre-vetted nonprofit providers eligible to receive future project-specific task orders, helping save up to seven months off a 13-month process. When approaching procurement, COGE took seriously its commitment to the labor force, both public and private sector, and specifically formulated recommendations that preserve all existing labor standards.
Prop 3: Fast-Track Street Safety Infrastructure Including Bike Lanes, Acquisition of Office Space and Activation of City Land
Speed up street safety projects, including bike lanes, by at least 33%
Reduce time for sale of small plots of City land from seven months to 90 days
Unlock more than $200 million in revenue for the City from sale of small surplus lots
The Commission’s third proposal streamlines how the City uses its own property. The Commission’s proposal would create a centralized permitting hub through which applicants could access and manage construction-related permits and approvals across City agencies. NYC DOT Launches Expansion of Curbside Electric Vehicle Charging Across Five Boroughs, Solicits Public Feedback on Locations

A car plugged into a curbside electric vehicle charger in the Bronx. Credit: NYC DOT
NEW YORK — New York City Department of Transportation (NYC DOT) Commissioner Mike Flynn today announced the launch of a new public online portal for New Yorkers to provide feedback on a major expansion of curbside electric vehicle (EV) charging. The expansion, part of NYC DOT’s PlugNYC charging network, will add 600 new curbside charging points over the next three years across all five boroughs, expanding from the current 88 Level 2 charging points that are heavily used across the city to nearly 700. The expansion will help support the city’s ambitious climate goals by facilitating the transition from fossil fuel-powered vehicles to electric vehicles. The transportation sector is one of the top contributors to greenhouse gas emissions in New York City. As part of the Mamdani administration’s vision for curb management that uses curb space to meet a wider range of public needs, the stations will also include “dig once” electrical connections for other uses where feasible, like e-bike battery charging.
“The Mamdani administration is taking aggressive action to address the climate crisis and achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. For the transportation sector, our main goal is making it easier and more affordable for New Yorkers to walk, bike, and take transit,” said NYC DOT Commissioner Mike Flynn . “But for those who still drive, we need to dramatically accelerate the adoption of electric vehicles. This initiative will make charging more affordable and convenient, helping support the transition from fossil fuel-powered vehicles to electric vehicles and expand charging access to TLC-licensed drivers and residents of low- and moderate-income neighborhoods underserved by private charging options.”
“The expansion of electric vehicle charging makes the transition to clean transportation more practical and accessible for New Yorkers. Passenger vehicles, including for-hire vehicles, account for 17% of New York City's greenhouse gas emissions, making vehicle electrification a critical part of achieving our climate goals,” said NYC Chief Climate Officer Louise Yeung . “By bringing curbside charging to neighborhoods that are home to many TLC-licensed drivers, we're reducing barriers to electric vehicle adoption while helping build a cleaner, healthier, and more equitable city.”
“These new chargers will be warmly welcomed by the tens of thousands of TLC drivers who are now conducting 3 million EV trips a month, as well as those who are eager to go electric and spare themselves some pain at the pump,” said NYC Taxi & Limousine Commissioner Midori Valdivia . “Being able to charge in the neighborhoods where they work and live not only saves our drivers time and money, but it also contributes to a cleaner, more sustainable future for everyone in our city.”
NYC DOT plans to install the new chargers over the next three years, in select neighborhoods based on high residential density, limited off-street parking, and a high concentration of TLC-licensed drivers, as well as input from TLC drivers themselves, starting with: Soundview and Unionport, in the Bronx; Rego Park, Forest Hills, and Far Rockaway, in Queens; Red Hook, Carroll Gardens, and Bedford-Stuyvesant, in Brooklyn; the Upper West Side and Washington Heights, in Manhattan; and Stapleton, on Staten Island, with more to come.
The expansion also seeks to provide access to public charging in low- and moderate-income communities where private charging companies have made few investments.
Public engagement, design, and construction will be rolling, and the public can comment on initial proposed sites at NYC DOT’s project portal, located at on.nyc.gov/plugnyc-expansion .
Each station will feature at least two charger posts, each with two charging points, for a total of four points per site. All stations will feature the PlugNYC brand. Where feasible, stations will also provide an electrical connection to support e-micromobility charging, such as electrified bikeshare docks, public e-bike charging cabinets, secure bike parking with charging, or cargo bike charging for microhubs. Site selection will be closely coordinated with the agency’s pedestrian, bike, public realm and bus projects to ensure future flexibility.
New York City launched a successful curbside L2 charging pilot in 2021 in partnership with Con Edison and has been operating this network of public L2 charging points since the end of the pilot in 2025. These existing curbside chargers, located in neighborhoods across the city, are immensely popular with EV owners: these 88 charging points are occupied by an EV over 70% of the time, in some cases close to 99% of the time, and actively delivering energy over 85% of the time they are occupied—the highest rate of utilization of any public curbside network in the country. Utilization is high across all neighborhood income levels, and four of the five highest utilized stations are in zip codes with high concentrations of TLC drivers, in Upper Manhattan, the Bronx and Queens.
EVs now account for 8.3% of new vehicle registrations in New York City, but to drive EV adoption higher will require addressing the city’s unique charging challenges. In typical American cities, most EV owners can charge in their own driveway or garage, but in New York, a high proportion of residents live in apartments or homes without off-street parking, and half of all car owners rely on street parking. Expanding public charging is therefore critical to unlocking greater EV adoption.
To address this gap as well as to support the New York City Taxi and Limousine Commission’s Green Rides initiative, NYC DOT is making major investments in fast charging and level 2 (L2) charging across the five boroughs, including this expansion of 600 curbside charging points, as well as 92 new fast charging points and 180 new L2 charging points at its City-owned Municipal Parking Fields and Municipal Parking Garages, with construction already underway or completed at many of those locations.
To install the new curbside charge points, NYC DOT will contract with the New York Power Authority, which is procuring the equipment and construction subcontractors best able to meet NYC DOT’s needs. NYC DOT will operate and maintain the new chargers as well as the existing charging stations from the pilot via contract, with control over the to-be-determined price charged to EV owners. Pricing will focus on affordability while encouraging reasonable turnover, and revenue received from customer fees will defray operating costs. Drivers will be able to use multiple apps or tap their credit card to pay per kilowatt hour of energy dispensed. Only EVs that are plugged in can legally park at a curbside EV charging station, and vehicles parked in these spaces and not connected to the charging station are subject to enforcement.

The co-hosts also ask Schwartz to recount the story about NYC’s first and short-lived on-street protected bike lane built soon after the 1980 transit strike. The lanes extended from Washington Square Park to Central Park, along Sixth Avenue in the northbound direction and a combination of Seventh Avenue/Broadway/Fifth Avenue riding southbound. After the lanes’ completion in 1980, their existence was doomed after Mayor Ed Koch ordered them removed. Koch had ridden in a presidential motorcade up Sixth Avenue with President Jimmy Carter and Governor Hugh Carey – where the governor had loudly complained to the president about them.
On the topic of congestion pricing, the discussion turns to the four East River bridges being tolled up until 1911, and a plan for tolling being approved in 1973 but killed when the mayoralty transitioned from John Lindsay to Abe Beame. The innovative street redesign builds on the success of the popular Open Street by prioritizing pedestrian and cyclist safety and improving connections and safety to Tompkins Square Park and nearby schools.
"The Avenue B Open Street has been a central meeting place for neighbors, families, and friends for years, serving as a connecting point to nearby schools, Tompkins Square Park, and other points through the East Village," said NYC DOT Commissioner Mike Flynn . Rendering showing the design for Avenue B with expanded pedestrian space and safety upgrades. Credit: NYC DOT
Through redesigns of intersections, new loading zones, and a series of one-way vehicle traffic reversals, NYC DOT will naturally reduce vehicle volumes along Avenue B to create a welcoming pedestrian corridor and a high-comfort two-way cycling experience. NYC DOT expects the project to be completed over the next several weeks, weather permitting.
The new design converts vehicle traffic to the northbound direction for a portion of the project area from East Seventh Street to East 10th Street. This naturally reduces traffic volumes to only necessary local access. Emergency vehicles and other city vehicles, like sanitation trucks, can still access Avenue B under the new design.
The agency will redesign every intersection in the project area with new pedestrian curb extensions that are protected with planters, bike corrals, granite blocks, or other elements.
These sidewalk expansions will provide the triple benefit of creating more public space for pedestrians and shortening crossing distances while also improving visibility between crossing pedestrians and other road users.
The agency will also install neighborhood loading zones at the start and end of every block to help reduce large truck traffic on the corridor. Vans are permitted to use the corridor for deliveries, loading, and unloading. Proposed “Click-To-Cancel" Rule

Work will remove the old concrete medians and curbs and clear overgrown or dead trees, weeds and accumulated debris. To ensure water flows into the medians, new inlet openings will be built to divert water off the streets and directly into the planted areas. Construction includes installing specially engineered soils and underground storage systems designed to absorb stormwater.
The landscaping will transform these spaces into green corridors, planted with native species, shrubs, grasses and wildflowers. Together, these improvements will cool and beautify the streetscape while providing stronger flood protection for surrounding neighborhoods. The projects also deliver landscape upgrades that enhance neighborhood aesthetic, while improving pedestrian safety with ADA‑compliant ramps and detectable warning surfaces.
Whitestone: Francis Lewis Boulevard, between 20th Avenue and 21st Road The medians along Francis Lewis Boulevard, now nearing completion, stretch 1,105 feet and capture runoff from a 1.17‑acre drainage area. They will manage 1.11 million gallons of stormwater each year, reducing combined sewer overflows (CSO) into the East River.
Forest Hills: Union Turnpike, between Metropolitan Avenue and 71st Road Set to be planted this fall, the Union Turnpike medians extend 3,245 feet and draw from a 2.56‑acre drainage area. They are designed to handle 2.43 million gallons of stormwater annually, easing pressure on the system and preventing CSO into Flushing Bay.
Queens Village: Springfield Boulevard, between Lucas Street and Merrick Boulevard Construction on Springfield Boulevard's medians, expected to begin later this year, will add 1,182 feet of green infrastructure tied to a 1.51‑acre drainage area. These improvements will manage 1.44 million gallons of stormwater each year and help limit CSO into Jamaica Bay.
